# What changed

Short notes for testing, newest first.

## The Bracket King and the No-Prep King have their own ladders (drag, part 4 of 4)

- **Bracket points:** every Bracket event you race (any class) puts its
  points on a bracket points list, yours and the field's. See it on the
  Career > Kings tab.
- **The King of the Track** (the last Sunday, noon-5 PM): finish 6 Bracket
  events and be in the top 8 on bracket points to get invited. Three
  rounds on dial-ins against three of the list's best regulars (in their
  top builds), then the Bracket King, for their car and $40,000. A loss
  ends your day: try again next month.
- **The no-prep top-10 list:** after 3 No-Prep events you can call out #10
  on no-prep nights (Saturday, 6 PM-1 AM): one callout a night, 1/8 from
  a dig, no times. Win and you take their spot (they move down); then call
  out anyone up to 2 spots above you. Each win pays 60% of that racer's
  class base. The list runs from class 3's quickest (#10) to class 6's
  (#1). Beat #1 and the No-Prep King races you on the last Friday night.
  A loss to the King locks you out till the next one.
- The week's agenda shows the King of the Track and your next callout.
- Try:
  - The Kings tab: the bracket points and the no-prep list.
  - On a Saturday night after 3 No-Prep events: call out #10.
  - After 6 Bracket events: the King of the Track on the last Sunday.
- Look out for: a callout you should be able to make but can't, the list
  shifting wrong after a win, the King of the Track field changing in the
  middle of the day.

## Sponsors (drag, part 3 of 4)

- **A new Sponsors tab** on the Career screen: your class sponsor, your
  associates, decals & contingency, and the track awards.
- **Class sponsor contracts now name ladder events** (for new signings;
  a contract you already signed keeps its terms):
  - the big check: win 2 Heads-Up events and 1 Bracket event;
  - the looks: win 2 strip events, reach the star rating, and show the
    car at a car show;
  - the street: win 1 No-Prep event and 2 street list nights.
  Still one per class, still needed before the King, no deadlines.
- **Associates: two decal slots** for brands of your class or earlier.
  Each pays every race day, plus a bonus when you win an event or finish
  runner-up (with their product on the car if they sell parts). Win 3
  events in a class with one on the car and it offers to be your class
  sponsor next class, on better terms (marked ★).
- **Decals & contingency:** parts brands (tires, turbos, intakes, race
  fuel...) pay when you win or finish runner-up with their product on the
  car and their decals on. Decals are free, one brand per kind of part,
  never a rival of your class sponsor's parts. Their parts discount grows
  with your wins: 5%, 10% after 5, 15% after 12.
- **Track awards** at Heads-Up and Bracket events: the quickest 60-foot of
  the day, and a hard-luck award for a first-round loser.
- All of it shows on the slip.
- Try:
  - Sign a class sponsor and read the contract. Win a Heads-Up and check
    the slip's "Counted for..." line.
  - Sign two associates and put on a tire brand's decals with drag tires
    on the car. Win, and check the slip's money lines.
  - Lose round 1 by a hair at a Bracket: the hard-luck award.
- Look out for: money that feels too generous (tell me and I'll tune it),
  a contract that won't count a win it should, decals you can't put on
  for no clear reason.

## Your drag tuner works the pit

- With a drag tuner on staff (Team), the pit has a new job: **Set it up
  for this track** (20 minutes). They set the tire pressure and the launch
  (2-step, launch throttle, boost in 1st and 2nd, traction control) for
  today's surface and temperature; a good chassis tuner (65+) does the
  shocks too. How close they get is their chassis skill. A no-prep night
  wants a different launch than a prepped strip by day.
- **After every pass they read it** (in the pit): it spun / shook / was
  clean, your 60-foot against your best today, what the hot engine cost.
  A better data skill reads more.
- **At a bracket race they call your dial** for the next round from
  today's passes, and it's filled in on the race-day screen (you can
  change it). A tuner who reads data badly is further off.
- Try: hire a drag tuner, set the tire pressure way off on purpose, enter
  an event and run a pass: read the pit, then "Set it up for this track".
  At a Bracket, check the dial-in after the time trials.
- Look out for: the setup taking more than a few seconds to work out on
  the phone, a dial that's clearly wrong for a good tuner.

## Race days by the clock, your pit, gear and trailers (drag, part 2 of 4)

- **A race day runs by the clock.** Enter an event and you're in your pit:
  the day's schedule (Heads-Up: qualifying at 10 AM and 12:30, round 1 at
  3 PM, then about every 45 minutes; Bracket and No-Prep have their own),
  the next call to the lanes and when the lanes close. **Miss a call and
  you're out** (in qualifying you just get no time from that pass). "Wait
  for the call" moves the clock; "To the lanes now" goes early.
- **The engine heats and cools by the minute.** About 40°F a pass, then
  down while you wait: the hood up is slowest, electric fans and an
  electric water pump faster, an engine chiller fastest. A hot engine
  loses a little power (up to about a tenth) and knocks sooner.
- **Breakage rules:** break in qualifying and can't fix it before round
  1? Take half the entry as track credit or the 20 points, and an
  alternate takes your spot. Blow an engine on the strip: a cleanup fine
  and 10 points. On the street a breakage ends the night.
- **New: Upgrade > Pit & Trailer** (the last tab): tools (Basic $350, Pro
  $1,800, Roll Cab $5,000), an engine chiller, a canopy, spare tires on
  their own wheels, axle shafts, spare nitrous bottles, and trailers. A
  second clutch or transmission on your shelf is a spare.
- **Load up when you enter:** pick how the car gets there (drive it, drive
  it pulling a small trailer, a flatbed, or your truck towing a car
  trailer) and what goes with it. It all has to fit: your trunk, the
  truck's bed, the trailer.
- **Pit work:** swap tires and bottles, replace an axle, a clutch or a
  transmission, fit parts you brought. Better tools and a better crew are
  faster. Engines and chassis are shop work.
- **Hire a mechanic** (Team > Mechanics): paid by the race day, and you
  can leave them home on cheap days.
- **Trailer hitch** (Parts > Weight & Safety). Small trailers go behind any
  car with a hitch; car trailers only behind a truck, within its tow
  rating.
- **The old $8,500 truck & trailer is gone**: if you had it, the money is
  back in your account (Ray texts you).
- The last section's Heads-Up and No-Prep have a **vendor row** (parts at
  shop prices, nitrous refills).
- Try:
  - Enter an event: the load-up screen, then the pit. Wait for a call, run,
    come back: the engine temperature and the next call.
  - Buy pro tools and an axle shaft, then if you snap an axle, fix it in
    the pit before the next call.
  - A trailer and a hitch: the extra room at the load-up.
  - Hire a mechanic and see the jobs go faster.
- Look out for: a call you couldn't make for no reason, a job that takes
  far too long or too short, the load-up not fitting on screen, money
  charged twice for the trip, and anything still mentioning the old truck
  & trailer.

## The career ladder (drag, part 1 of 4)

The drag career is rebuilt. **Old saves carry over** (classes you finished
stay finished; in your current class each old race day you won counts as
one event done), but if anything looks wrong, start a new career.

- **Each class is now 5 sections of 4 events**, then the King event:
  - **Heads-Up** (Saturday 8 AM-6 PM, prepped strip): 2 qualifying passes
    seed a 16-car ladder (quickest vs slowest), then 4 rounds.
  - **Bracket** (Sunday 8 AM-6 PM): 2 time trials, then 4 rounds on
    dial-ins (break out and you lose).
  - **No-Prep** (Saturday 6 PM-1 AM, 1/8 from a dig): a chip draw every
    round, 4 rounds, no times shown: only who won.
  - **Street list night** (Friday 10 PM-3 AM, 1/8): 4-7 callouts, one race
    each; you can lose at most one.
- **The three eliminations must be won outright: every round.** Win 3 of a
  section's 4 events to open the next section.
- **Every event runs again each week** at its time slot, for its money and
  points. Purses pay by the round you reached (the winner most).
- **The fields are set in advance:** about 20 named regulars per class,
  each with their own car that gets quicker section by section. Some
  events are restricted (Stock Motor, 275 Radial, Small Block...): everyone
  in them runs to the rule.
- **Standings** (new tab): 20 points for racing, +10 per round won, +1 for
  the win, against the class's regulars. Beat the King while in the top 3
  for a standings bonus.
- **The King event** (2nd and 4th Saturday nights, 9 PM-midnight): the 5
  ladder rivals one at a time, then the King. Your sponsor contract still
  has to be done first. A loss ends that night; try again on the next one.
- **Parts and cars unlock with the first win of each event** (the event
  card says what it opens).
- Gone: rep, the old callout ladder, side brackets and race-day scoring
  (all folded into the ladder). Car clubs, wanderers, street spots, Grudge
  Night, car shows, the promoter, pink slips and the short series stay.
- Street events need no license.
- Try:
  - The Career screen: the Strip, King and Standings tabs, and the road of
    sections.
  - A whole Heads-Up day: check the qualifying slips, your seed, the
    round names, and that the winner's money lands.
  - A Bracket day (your dial-in on the slip) and a No-Prep night (no times
    on the slip).
  - A street list night: lose one and keep going, lose two and it's over.
  - An old save: are your finished classes still done?
- Look out for: a field or card with a missing car or name, an event that
  won't open in its time slot, money that looks too high or too low, and
  anything that still mentions rep.

## Day or night, same engine; tuners respect the knock point; alcohol boost limits

- **The weather no longer changes the engine.** Day or night it makes the
  same power, knocks at the same boost and carries the same engine risk.
  The air and track temperature still change grip (a cold night track grips
  less). Back-to-back race rounds still heat-soak the engine a little (the
  race intro says "heat-soaked" when it costs you).
- **Running past the knock point is less risky, and the risk grows the
  deeper you go.** Just past it, the tune pulls timing and it's safe; the
  risk climbs fast toward where power stops (3% a pass right there).
  Detonating past that is as risky as before. Your LS1 at 32.5 psi on E85
  drops from about 8% to well under 1%.
- **Hired tuners:**
  - Safe stops at the knock point (no risk).
  - Balanced runs about a third of the way past it.
  - On the edge runs about 60% of the way.
  - None of them adds boost past the knock point that buys no power (a
    turbo out of breath): they say so in the dyno notes.
  - The tuner's notes end with the engine risk the tune leaves and why
    ("Leaves it at 0.9% engine risk a pass: 7 psi past the knock point").
- **Methanol (and E85 / race gas on older engines) takes more boost**,
  matching real engines: a 454 on methanol knocks past ~46 psi (was 33),
  12.25:1 methanol ~39 psi (was 21). Pump gas didn't change. Rivals and
  packages didn't change.
- Try:
  - Your E85 car: check the risk on the Tune screen at your old boost.
  - A tuner session at each aggression: read the last dyno notes and the
    risk line in the tuner's notes.
  - A night race: the intro shouldn't mention cool air or hot charge.

## Paying rent

- Rent (and Ray's $400 once you're past class 2, and storage) comes out of
  your cash by itself on the 1st. If you're short that day it's owed, and
  until now there was no way to pay it before the next 1st.
- Now there's a **Pay** button for anything owed: Home screen under Bills,
  the Bank, and the Move house window. The garage's Home tile turns red
  ("Owed $1,540: tap to pay") while you owe.
- The Home screen's Bills also says how it works: paid by itself on the 1st,
  pay what's owed any time.
- Try: be short on the 1st, check the Home tile in the garage, tap it, pay.

## The shop says when brakes block a wheel size

- On cars with big factory brakes (the '18 Mustang GT needs an 18 in wheel
  up front), smaller wheels don't show for that axle, and the shop didn't
  say why. Now:
  - the wheel list says the smallest wheel over your brakes and what
    Lightweight Drag Brakes allow (Mustang front: 18 in, or 15 in with drag
    brakes);
  - in the tire list's other sizes, sizes your brakes block say "needs drag
    brakes";
  - a tire that can't go on any wheel over your brakes says so, and names
    the fix.
- Try: Mustang GT '18 with a 17 in tire up front. The warning should point
  to Brakes; fit the Lightweight Drag Brakes and the 17 in front wheels
  appear.

## Fix: tires unlocked on one axle but not the other

- Track and road-race tires (circuit unlocks) and the drag tires showed as
  open on the non-driven axle but locked on the drive axle. A tire now
  unlocks the same on both axles.

## Drag springs & shocks: front-drive wording

- Drag Coilovers and Adjustable Drag Shocks worked on front-drive cars, but
  their text (and the tuning help) only explained rear-drive cars. On a
  FWD car they now say what helps there: stiff at the back so it doesn't
  squat, softer front springs, firm rear rebound. (Measured: each worth a
  few hundredths on a big FWD car.)

## Wheel wells on every car, front-drive suspension and axles

- **Rolled & Pulled Fenders on every car**, front and rear (Suspension >
  Front / Rear Wheel Wells): about an inch more room for wider tires. Tubs
  stay for rear-drive and AWD cars; the one-piece front end stays for
  front-drive cars. A GT-R with fenders all round fits 315/40R18 radials.
- **Front-drive cars get their own suspension parts:** Stiff Rear Springs &
  Shocks and a Rear Lockout (the rear can't squat, the weight stays on the
  front tires: about 0.2 s on a big FWD car; drag only, it hurts in
  corners), and a Front Traction Bar (stops wheel hop). Traction bars,
  ladder bars and 4-links no longer show on FWD cars (they did nothing).
- **Front-drive axles:** the axle slot is Axles (Halfshafts) on FWD cars:
  Level 2.9, Level 5.9 and Pro-Level axles. The driveshaft slot is gone on
  FWD cars (they don't have one).
- **Automatics:** a lighter flexplate does very little (the converter is
  the big spinning mass), as on real cars.
- **Circuit races:** the differential matters now: a limited-slip is
  quickest, an open diff spins off the corners, a spool pushes.
- **Your saves:** a FWD car with traction bars / ladder bars / a 4-link or
  a driveshaft has them moved to your shelf; its axles become the matching
  halfshaft level.
- **Try:** a FWD car's Suspension and Drivetrain sections; fenders on any
  car; a spool vs a limited-slip on the circuit.
- Rivals unchanged.

- **Fix:** factory tires now say their real size (a Civic's 185/60R14 said
  it fits a 15 in wheel).
- Lamborghini and Viper finally get a drag radial in the tire packages
  (305/35R20).
- **Try:** a Civic's Tires slot (nothing fits its 14s: the other sizes open
  by themselves), a Fox on 15s, a GT-R or Camaro SS (18 in brakes).

## Weight reduction checked against real cars

- **Weight Reduction stages now save what the real work saves** (they were
  about half at the cheap end). A Fox Mustang: Spare/Jack/A-C 90 lb,
  Interior Strip 219, Lightweight Panels 387, Carbon & Lexan 490 (was 55 /
  139 / 276 / 441). A Civic: 66 / 161 / 284 / 360. The shop text says what
  each stage takes out.
- **Roll bar and cage weigh what real ones do:** 8-point bar 66 lb (was
  40), 25.3 chromoly cage 93 lb (was 57).
- **Race Accessory Delete:** -8 kg now; the A/C was being counted twice
  (it comes out with Weight Reduction).
- **Pro Mod and dragster:** they're already tube-chassis race cars at
  class weight, so weight reduction, cages, tube chassis and battery moves
  no longer show for them. Anything fitted is moved to your shelf to sell.
- **Try:** a mid-build car with Stage 1 or 2: about 0.03-0.13 s quicker.
  A very powerful rear-drive car that already spins can be a touch slower
  stripped (less weight on the rear tires, as on a real car).
- Rivals unchanged.

## Front-drive drag cars

- **Front Wheel Wells** (Suspension, front-drive cars only): Rolled & Pulled
  Fenders ($600, room for the new **Drag Slick 26x10**) and a Wide One-Piece
  Front End ($6,000, room for a 28x10.5 slick). Rear tubs no longer show on
  front-drive cars (they did nothing there).
- **Weight stays on the front:** the trunk battery is gone from front-drive
  cars; instead a **Lightweight Race Battery (Front)** saves 12 kg in the
  engine bay. New **Front Ballast** (25 or 50 kg behind the bumper): more
  load on the drive tires, more weight to move. Weight reduction on a
  front-drive car now strips the back half, so the car gets more
  front-heavy (Stage 3: about 64% front instead of 60%).
- **Wheelie bars on front-drive cars** now do something: set low (Tuning:
  0 to 1 degree), they hold the nose down and keep weight on the front
  tires.
- **Slider clutch:** base pressure now goes down to 10% and lockup time up
  to 4 s, so a big turbo car can launch without flash-spinning the tires.
- **Packages:** front-drive Race/Extreme tire packages bring the fender work
  and the bigger slick; the Extreme drivetrain package no longer converts a
  front-drive car to rear drive (the RWD conversion is still in the shop).
- **Try:** a Civic, Integra, Neon, GTI or Cobalt with power: fit the fenders
  and the 26x10, the front battery, ballast and wheelie bars at 0-0.5
  degrees, and the slider clutch low (15-20%, 3-4 s). A 1,500 hp Civic went
  from 10.3 to about 8.8 in testing. Look out for: the bigger slick wants a
  new launch rpm (try lower); anything odd with the bars or the battery
  options on rear-drive and AWD cars (they should be unchanged).
- Real 1,500 hp front-drive Civics run 7.8 with a 1.35 60-foot; we're at
  about 8.8 / 1.70. Next step on that is a question for the owner.
- Fix: Full Tubs now really take off the 10 kg they promise.
- Rivals unchanged.

## AWD tire packages fixed

- **Wheels & Tires packages on AWD cars now fit both axles with the same
  tire** (an AWD driveline wants the same size front and rear). Before,
  they only changed the rear tires, and the factory fronts made the car
  slower than stock (an R34 on the Extreme tires lost 0.14 s).
- **Try:** an Evo, STI, R34, GT-R, 911 Turbo or the Ram diesel: buy a tire
  package and check both Front and Rear Tires change, and the car is no
  slower than before.
- On a stock-power AWD car, bigger tires don't help much (it isn't short of
  grip yet): the gain comes with power.
- The Extreme package can now fall back to a 275/40R17 drag radial on cars
  with big brakes (the 911 got nothing before).
- Rivals unchanged. FWD traction is next.

## Wheels & tires rework

- **Four slots: Front Wheels, Front Tires, Rear Wheels, Rear Tires**
  (Upgrade > Wheels & Tires). Pick a wheel style, then its size (diameter x
  width); tires come in real sizes and go on a wheel of their diameter and
  width. The shop ticks what fits and offers the other half to buy with it.
- **Wheels:** Street 5-Spoke, Forged Lightweight, Drag Wheel (with skinny
  front-runner sizes) and Beadlock Drag, each in its real sizes and
  weights, drawn on the car. They add show stars (Style > Wheels links
  there).
- **Tires:** new 275/60R15 and 225/50R15 drag radials, a 305/45R18 for Brembo
  cars, radial slicks (28x10.5R15, 31x13.5R15: steady pass after pass on a
  prepped track, poor on no-prep), a 24.5x8 slick for front-drive cars,
  and front runners for 15, 17 and 18 in wheels.
- **What sizes do:** a 15 in wheel's taller sidewall softens the hit; a
  wider wheel steadies the tire (less shake, radials take more pressure);
  bias slicks grow taller down track.
- **Fitment:** big brakes need big wheels (drag brakes clear 15s); wide rear
  tires need tubs (Suspension > Rear Wheel Tubs: mini $2,500, full $6,000).
  A car whose wheels and tires don't fit can't race until it's fixed.
- **AWD:** the front tires drive too: front runners on an AWD car now cost
  grip (no more free ET).
- **Packages:** Wheels & Tires packages pick the biggest tire of each tier
  that fits the car's wells and brakes.
- **Your saves:** drive tires get wheels they fit (beadlocks stay beadlocks),
  front runners become runners on runner wheels; a build that can't fit any
  more (e.g. carbon brakes with 15 in beadlocks, a big slick under stock
  wells) stays on but is flagged until you change it.

**Try:** Upgrade > Wheels & Tires on a few cars (a Fox, a Civic, a Mustang
'18); a slick with tubs; the Style > Wheels link. **Look out for:** a size
that should fit but isn't ticked, wheels that look wrong on a car, a flag
you don't understand.

## Tuner pricing, diesel head studs

- **Dyno work is always paid now.** "No gain, no pay" covers the track work
  only. Before, a dyno-only booking was always refunded in full (and said it
  found nothing) while the dyno tune stayed on the car. A dyno-only booking
  now costs what the quote says, plus the check pass's ticket and fuel (no
  track day fee).
- **Head studs on a diesel:** they used to *lower* a diesel's boost limit
  (45 -> 40 psi). Now ARP studs hold about 65 psi on a diesel and the
  O-ringed block about 100 (gas engines unchanged). The shop text gives the
  diesel numbers.

**Try:** book a tuner for the Engine only, then Engine + Launch, and check the
money; studs on the diesel truck with a big turbo (boost limit in Setup).

## Diesel fuel setting

- **New: Fuel (Setup > Engine) on a tuned diesel**, 60-100%. On a diesel the
  fuel makes the power: turn it down for a cooler exhaust and less strain on
  the engine and gearbox; turn it up for power. The Setup panel now shows the
  exhaust temperature (amber past 1,450 F, red past 1,700 F). Needs a diesel
  tune (handheld or race); stays at 100% unless you change it.
- **The tuner on a diesel** runs the boost up to what the head gasket holds,
  then turns the fuel down pull by pull until the exhaust and internals are
  happy (Safe ~1,450 F, Balanced ~1,550 F, On the edge ~1,650 F). Example:
  the truck with race tune, 100% injectors and the stock-size VGT went from
  775 hp at 1,800 F (engine risk) to 680 hp at 1,510 F.
- **Fixed (tuner):** when the track work didn't pay off, the tuner put back
  the tune from before the dyno, undoing the dyno's work (a fuel cut, or
  boost brought back from the knock point). Now only the track changes are
  undone; the dyno tune stays.

**Try:** the diesel truck with the race tune and bigger injectors: move the
Fuel slider and watch power and EXHAUST; book a tuner (Engine) and read the
dyno notes. **Look out for:** the Fuel slider on a gas car (it shouldn't be
there), or a tuner session that leaves a diesel's exhaust red.

## Big turbos for built engines, blowers that fit, nitrous spools turbos, safe diesel packages

- **The shop now sells the turbos a built engine can use:** a 4G63 or SR20
  can buy the 9498, a 2JZ the twin 7685s, an LS the Pro Mod twins. On an
  engine that isn't built yet the spec sheet says so ("too big for this
  engine as it is ... it comes alive built: race heads, cams, valvetrain and
  bottom end"). The record power from the engine-ceilings update is
  reachable now.
- **Superchargers that fit the engine:** no more 14-71 Pro Mod blower for a
  1.0 three-cylinder. Big blowers show on big engines, small ones on small.
  A blower already on your car still shows.
- **Nitrous lights a turbo:** spraying brings a laggy turbo on boost much
  sooner (a 150 shot on a 2JZ with an 8891: full boost about 2.5 s sooner).
- **Diesel power packages are safe now:** they run the turbo to what the
  head gasket holds and match the injectors to the turbo; no more 1,800 F
  exhaust. Pro and Extreme power: about 600 hp on a stock bottom end; the
  full Extreme build about 850 hp.
- **Diesel tuner:** says when the exhaust runs hot and what fixes it.

**Try:** a stock Evo or S13 in the turbo shop (big turbos listed, with the
note); a turbo car with a nitrous shot; a diesel power package. **Look out
for:** a turbo or blower listed for an engine where it makes no sense.

## Audit fixes: diesel warnings, dyno with sound off

- **Fixed: the dyno could crash with the sound off** (or before sound had
  started): the Pull button froze the game. Same for the circuit screen.
- **Shop texts:** race valve springs and oiling now say what they really
  give on engines built to rev (a 4G63's Pro Mod valvetrain: 3,900 rpm more,
  not 2,300).
- **Diesels:** no more "Misfiring" or ignition warnings at big boost (a
  diesel has no spark); the shop's status no longer raises a diesel's
  failure risk every time it's shown; the dyno and data logger show a
  diesel's real air-fuel ratio (16-25:1, not gasoline numbers); water-meth
  is no longer offered on diesels (it did nothing there yet).

**Try:** the dyno with sound off; a race-built Cummins on the dyno.
**Look out for:** any screen that freezes.

## Audit fixes: parts vanishing from the shelf, overdriven blowers

- **Fixed: spare parts could disappear from your shelf when the game
  loaded.** It checked each spare part against whatever car was active, so
  a transaxle off a front-driver, diesel parts, carb fuel pumps or a
  conversion kit for another car could be deleted. Now they stay. (Parts
  already lost before this can't be brought back.)
- **Fixed: a supercharger turned far past what the engine takes on its
  fuel** made almost no power (the dyno could even show negative power).
  It now makes about what the engine does without boost while it detonates,
  with the same high chance of breaking.

**Try:** park a car with spare parts on the shelf, switch to a different
kind of car, close and reopen the game. **Look out for:** anything missing
from the shelf.

## Nitrous on a boosted engine knocks sooner

- Spraying on boost, the tune pulls timing for the shot (about 2 degrees
  per 50 hp, more on a small engine), so the engine knocks sooner while it
  sprays: a 150 shot on a 2JZ takes about 3 psi off its knock point on
  pump gas. The Setup screen, warnings and race intro say how much; tuners
  run a little less boost with a big shot on.
- Nitrous on its own (no boost) and diesels are unchanged.

**Try:** a turbo car with a 150 shot on pump gas, boost near its knock
point. **Look out for:** warnings that don't match the shot or the fuel.

## Knock follow-up: weather counts, logger shows it, a clutch bug fixed

- **Hot days knock sooner in races.** The day's air temperature (and an
  engine heat-soaked between rounds) moves the knock point: about 2 psi
  less on a 100°F day than a 77°F one, more on a cool night. The race intro
  tells you when it matters ("hot charge: knocks past ~14 psi today").
- **Data logger:** the Timing pulled trace now includes timing pulled for
  knock, and the Pro logger tells you how much and from where.
- **Fixed: a clutch that couldn't hold the engine's torque lost half the
  drive** (the car could even get quicker with less power). Now it slips
  but still passes everything it can hold. Cars on a clutch too small for
  their power are quicker; a few rivals are up to 0.05 s quicker.
- Diesels no longer show knock warnings at big boost.

**Try:** a boosted car on a hot day vs a cool night; a big-power car on its
stock clutch. **Look out for:** anything that gets quicker with less power.

## Knock: how much boost depends on the engine, not just the fuel

- **No more one number per fuel.** Each engine has its real compression
  ratio (shown on its spec) and head design. On 93 a low-compression turbo
  engine takes about 20 psi (2JZ-GTE, RB26, SR20, Evo ~22), a 10:1 engine
  about 15, an LS ~13, a Coyote ~12, an S2000 ~8. A hot charge takes some
  off; E85, race gas and methanol take about 2x / 2x / 4x as much.
- **No hard cut-off.** Past that point the tune pulls timing: each psi adds
  less, and about 30% further on power stops climbing (the exhaust gets hot
  and the boost creeps up a little). Past that it detonates: power drops
  and the engine is at risk.
- Knock is worst at peak torque and eases as the revs rise.
- **Pistons set compression:** low-comp forged about -1.5 points (4-5 psi
  more on pump gas), high-comp +1 point (3 psi less, +4% power).
- **Dyno:** the boost line turns orange where timing is being pulled, with
  the rpm range listed. The Setup screen says where your engine knocks and
  where power stops. Tuners now push into the pulled zone like real ones.
- Stock cars run the same. Boosted builds change power (low-compression
  engines gain, high-compression ones lose some). A few rivals are a touch
  quicker. Also fixed the dyno showing "7888.888888 rpm".

**Try:** your turbo car on the dyno, then turn the boost up 2 psi at a time
and watch where the orange starts and where power stops. **Look out for:**
an engine that takes far more or less boost than you'd expect for it.

## Engine power ceilings: full race builds reach real record power

- **Race fuels hold more boost:** methanol 80 psi (was 50), E85 40 (30),
  race gas 40 (34). Pump gas stays 17 psi (that's right for 93 octane with
  an intercooler).
- **Race turbos turn up further:** 8891 to 70 psi, 9498 to 80, twin 7685s
  to 60, Pro Mod twins to 80.
- **Engines famous for big power now make it** on a full race build: SR20
  and 4G63 about 2,000 hp, RB26 ~2,140, 2JZ-GTE ~2,350, Buick turbo / GNX
  ~1,500, 3S ~1,500, VR38 ~2,450. They also rev higher with race valvetrain
  and billet parts (4G63 10,500, SR20 10,000, RB26 10,200).
- **A few engines that ran too strong at high boost are trimmed** to real
  numbers: 4A-G, the 3.2 I6, VQ35, 3800, V10, V12.
- **The Cummins goes further:** race injectors and the triple CP3 kit feed
  about 2,600 hp, race triples to 100 psi: about 2,300 hp.
- Nothing changes up to 15 psi: stock cars and mild builds are as before.
  Builds past 15 psi change power (up or down by engine). Some rivals'
  times move; the career rebalance will sort them out.

**Try:** a full race build on an SR20, 4G63, RB26 or 2JZ on methanol with
the 9498 or twin 7685s, boost turned up. **Look out for:** power that feels
wrong for an engine you know, or a build of yours that lost a lot.

## Fix: big single turbos on V6/V8 engines hold their boost up top

- A big single turbo on a V engine (a GT-R or LS with an 8891, say) lost
  too much exhaust energy in its crossover pipes at full power, so it
  couldn't hold boost near redline (a GT-R with the 8891 held 30-35 psi,
  about 1,340 hp). Now the loss is big at low flow (where it slows spool,
  as before) and small flat out: that GT-R holds 50 psi from ~6,000 rpm and
  makes its turbo's ~1,750 hp. Spool on V8s moves only 100-200 rpm.
- Rivals barely move (at most 0.015 s).

**Try:** a V6/V8 with a big single turbo on the dyno: boost should hold to
the limiter. **Look out for:** spool that now feels too early.

## AWD burnouts: one quick hit

- AWD cars prep their tires like real AWD drag cars: **one quick hit**. Hold
  GAS: it revs on the brakes (on the 2-step, building boost, if it has one),
  then **launches flat out through the water with all four spinning**, like
  off the line, for about a second, brakes to a stop and pulls up to the
  line by itself (it backs up if the hit carries it past the line).
- Traction control and launch-clutch slip are off for the hit, so it really
  spins; the tires keep the heat it puts in (about 15-25 C). Smoke off all
  four. Rival AWD cars do the same.

**Try:** an Evo, GT-R, STI or 911, stock and with a 2-step. **Look out for:**
a hit that hooks instead of spinning, the car ending up in the wrong place,
or the race starting with the wrong speed or gear.

## Diesel rework: fuel makes the power, smoke, EGT, G56, Cummins swaps

- **The Cummins works like a real diesel now.** Fuel makes the power: the
  injectors, the pump and the tune set how much goes in; the turbo's air
  decides how cleanly it burns. Short of air it **rolls black smoke**, the
  exhaust runs hot and the extra fuel gives less and less. More fuel also
  lights a bigger turbo sooner, like the real thing.
- **Real stock numbers:** 370 hp / 800 lb-ft (16.0 in the quarter, like a
  real crew-cab 4x4). The factory computer limits torque in 1st and 2nd.
- **Diesel parts** (only on a diesel; gas injectors, pumps, gas turbos,
  blow-off valves, wastegates and 2-steps are gone from it): injectors 25%
  to 400% over, lift pump / stroker / dual / triple CP3, a Diesel Tune
  (+~130 hp) and a Race Tune (no smoke limiter), Drop-in VGT, S366, S480,
  **S366/S480 compounds** and race triples, diesel converters (2,200 /
  2,600 / 3,000 stall, nothing looser), built 68RFE, built 48RE, South Bend
  clutches. Packages pick diesel parts too.
- **EGT gauge** on the race dash for diesels: green, amber past 1,300 F, red
  past 1,600 F. Past ~1,700 F the engine-risk box warns (pistons, turbo).
- **G56 manual** at the dealer ($2,695 less): like the real truck its engine
  is detuned (350 hp / 660 lb-ft). A diesel tune unlocks it, then the stock
  clutch slips until you fit a dual disc.
- **Cummins swaps:** the 6.7 is $12,500 in the engine list, plus a $3,500
  swap kit (and fabrication on smaller cars). Behind it: the G56, 68RFE,
  48RE or a race box (TH400, Powerglide, Lenco). Diesel parts follow the
  engine.
- **Diesel Dan** (docks, Mondays) now really races a Cummins: coal-rolling,
  about 13.5 on the street.
- Your truck's old gas parts became the nearest diesel ones.

**Try:** the stock Ram, then a Diesel Tune, then 100% injectors on the stock
turbo (watch the smoke and the EGT), then add an S366 or compounds. A G56
truck with a tune, then a dual-disc clutch. **Look out for:** smoke that
never stops or never shows, an EGT gauge that seems off, a part that's
missing on the truck or showing on a gas car.

## Turbo rework: real spool, new turbo lineup, spec sheets

- **Turbos now come on boost the way real ones do.** The game works out
  spool from your engine and the turbo together: displacement, how high it
  revs, how well it breathes (heads and cams count), exhaust heat, and the
  turbo's own wheels and housing. A big turbo on a small engine is lazy, the
  same turbo on a big V8 lights early; a single turbo on a V8 (long crossover
  pipes) spools later than twins. Boost also has to spin the turbo up, so
  big wheels lag more and ball bearings recover faster after a shift.
- **New lineup, named like the real ones** (compressor/turbine wheel sizes):
  singles 5057, 4847, 5558, 6266, 7275, 7685, 8891, 9498; twins 4847, 6266,
  7685 and the 9498 Pro Mod pair. **Single** and **Twin** sections in the
  shop, and only the sizes that suit your engine show up. Twins need 6+
  cylinders. Your old turbo becomes the nearest new one (61mm -> 6266, 80mm
  -> 7685, the others keep their slot).
- **Spec sheet:** tap a turbo in the shop (or **Turbo specs** on the dyno):
  wheel sizes, housing, bearing, max boost, the power it supports, and on
  your engine where boost starts and where it's full, with a boost chart and
  warnings (too big / runs out of air).
- **Factory turbos are the real ones** (TD05 twin-scroll on the Evo, VF48
  on the STI, CT20B sequential twins on the Supra, HT-12 sequentials on the
  RX-7, VTG on the 911, HE300VG on the Ram...). Most stock cars now run
  closer to their real timeslips.
- **Manifolds tell the truth:** the cast log answers quickest; tubular and
  equal-length make more top end but spool a touch later. **New: Twin-Scroll
  Divided Manifold** ($2,600, fours and inline sixes): a few hundred rpm
  sooner, chokes a little sooner at big power. Wastegates are boost control
  only now (no "spools sooner").
- **Rivals changed too:** about half the turbo rivals' times moved (most by
  a few hundredths; some bosses are up to ~0.4 s quicker). A career balance
  pass comes next.

**Try:** Upgrade > Turbo / Supercharger: open a few turbos and read the spec
sheet on your car; fit one and do a dyno pull. **Look out for:** a turbo
that should suit your engine but doesn't show up, spool numbers that feel
off for a combo you know, or a rival that's suddenly out of reach.

## Manual burnout shifting, pick your gearbox at the dealer

- **Manual cars shift in the burnout.** While you hold GAS in the box, the

  real option price (e.g. '18 Mustang GT 10-speed auto +$1,595, Hellcat
  6-speed manual -$1,995, Chevelle Turbo 400 auto +$250). The one you pick is
  the car's factory gearbox. Cars you already own don't change.
- **Free sponsor car:** the same gearbox choice, and your sponsor pays for
  either one.

**Try:** a manual car on slicks: hold GAS, tap SHIFT once or twice, let off,
tap STAGE. **Look out for:** a STAGE tap that shifts instead (it only shifts
while your finger is on the gas), or the button label not switching back. Buy a car with the other gearbox
and check Upgrade > Transmission says it's the factory one.
